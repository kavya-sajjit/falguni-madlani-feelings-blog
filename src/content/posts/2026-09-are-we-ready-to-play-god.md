<p>&nbsp;<span style="text-align: center;">&nbsp;Are we ready to play God !</span></p><p>Yesterday I was thrilled by listening to the news of dire wolves being de-extincted. Being a biologist I was very much happy to see the uses of biotechnology in conservation and protection of nature. Looking at the video of those two puppies Romulus and Remus dancing around was a thrill unmatched as I became a witness to the creation of history.</p><p>Texas based Colossal Biosciences created dire wolf pups that went extinct nearly 12500 years<span> ago through genetic engineering. They used DNA from a 13000 year old tooth and a 72000 year old skull, did some gene editing with the DNA of present day grey wolf, some culture gowth inlab, surrogate mother and Voila... new species resurrected from the past. Perfectly in a sci-fi style except that this turned out to be fact. Check out the video here<a href="https://youtu.be/F5uCuOwK_VE?si=yN0Fu1U__zvoE79a" target="_blank">https://youtu.be/F5uCuOwK_VE?si=yN0Fu1U__zvoE79a</a>&nbsp;</span></p><p><span>I have devored this news hungrily as I found it amazing but is it that simple or is that more to it? How does one draw the line at creating new species? Will these researches serve lofty purposes and rise to the occasion of Science and mankind? Or it becomes a source for making designer babies? Questions Galore. </span>The trial of creating dire wolf itself has come from the populist craze of Game of Thrones. May be Science also likes to pander to the entertainment industry.</p><p>But Colossal is stating that it is committed to use their technology to contribute to conservation of threatened species, edit harmful genes out of the population of threatened species and introducing traits to help animals adapt to environmental change.</p><p><span style="font-family: inherit;">Hopefully this experiment benefits our planet and not lead to a Jurassic park kind of situation. If Romulus and Romeus can live in wild, then it is a great leap forward. But, what if they doesn't belong. It has taken million of years for nature to arrive at where it stands. Tinkering with it may lead to both ways- rapid downfall or adapting to our changing ecosystem.</span></p><p><span style="font-family: inherit;">The idea of playing God is deep rooted in humans. But instead of becoming ready to play God, shouldn't we be ready to be better humans?</span></p><p><span style="font-family: inherit;"><br /></span></p><p><span style="font-family: inherit;">Falguni Madlani</span></p><p>&nbsp;</p><div><br /></div>
