<p>&nbsp;<span style="text-align: center;">Uff yeh Exams - part II</span></p><p style="text-align: justify;"><span>&nbsp;&nbsp; &nbsp;</span><span>&nbsp;&nbsp; &nbsp;</span><span>&nbsp;&nbsp; &nbsp;</span><span>&nbsp;&nbsp; &nbsp;</span><span>&nbsp;&nbsp; &nbsp;</span><span>&nbsp; &nbsp;&nbsp;</span>Again March is around the corner. Our educational institutions along with all the stake holders,&nbsp; teachers, students, tution teachets, parents enter into a state of flurry and are busy like a bee.&nbsp;</p><p style="text-align: justify;">Many of us as teachers interact with teachers and students of other institutions and have varied kind of experiences.&nbsp;</p><p style="text-align: justify;">During one of my such interactions,&nbsp; I was posed a question " Do the means justify the end?" Is obtaining full marks the only goal? Shouldn't the journey or the process of learning be also important and to be enjoyed?</p><p style="text-align: justify;">In this rat race for marks, are we deviating from the actual goal of learning...like developing the values of hardwork, honesty, joy of learning and knowledge on the path of getting degrees. Or we are just happy to take all shortcuts and are greedy only for marks,marks,marks?</p><p style="text-align: justify;">I want to narrate a small story which may help us to think.</p><p style="text-align: justify;">Once upon a time when Buddhism and Jainism was on its rise in India, there lived a king named Mahendravarma. He was greatly influenced by the teachings of Buddha and wanted to practice ahimsa. But at that time animal sacrifices was very prevalent in his kingdom.</p><p style="text-align: justify;">King Mahendravarma took out a ruling to the effect that all animal sacrifices in his kingdom would be immediately stopped henceforth.&nbsp;</p><p style="text-align: justify;">Since he was a very just king and widely loved and respected by his citizens, the people of his kingdom obeyed his rule. But there was this one sect of people in his kingdom who believed in animal sacrifice and they were against Mahendra Verma because he stopped animal sacrifice. They held a meeting and decided to hold to an animal sacrifice openly in defiance of the king. They decided to hold a huge celebration and sacrifice a goat. They called all the like minded people to join them. This news reached Mahendra Verma and he became heartbroken and sad as some of his subjects were not following his rule.</p><p style="text-align: justify;">He came to a decision and declared to his Council of Ministers that in case a single drop of animal blood is spilled in his kingdom, he would sacrifice himself. On hearing this, the chief minister thundered that he will take care that nothing of the sort happens and he would teach those dissidents a lesson.</p><p style="text-align: justify;">&nbsp;But Mahendra Verma refused and said no he would like his people to listen to him voluntarily and not by coercion. The opposing group of people were repare for the day of sacrifice they started announcing that in the whole Kingdom that they are going to do an animal sacrifice on the Amavasya of that month. The whole kingdom started to discuss the possibiities and the outcome of the day.</p><p style="text-align: justify;">As Amavasya started approaching near, apprehensions, arguments, discussions started increasing. The day of Amavasya came....A huge gathering of people left the temple in a procession with a goat leading the procession with a garland around the neck. The chief protestor, a tantrik chanting mantras with drums and trumpet bearers following them. They were going to the ground exactly in front of the palace to challenge Mahendravarma.</p><p style="text-align: justify;">Mahendravarma was pacing restlessly in the palace corridors with thoughts teeming in his mind.The council of ministers too in full attendance stood by the side only with the major commander in chief of the army and chief minister missing. They were trying to make the protestors listen but in vain.</p><p style="text-align: justify;">The sound of drums became louder and louder indicating the procession getting nearer. The crowd started swelling, some in support, some against, some curious, some bystanders.</p><p style="text-align: justify;">The dchants, drums, trumpets rose to a freny indicating the time of sacrifice and along with it Mahendravarma drew out his sword ready to sacrifice himself in case he fails to achieve ahimsa. He believed in the goodness of his subjects, but the chants becameloder and louder.</p><p style="text-align: justify;">Suddenly the sound stopped followed by a defeaning silence and a stream of blood came out from the doors of the hall on to the road in front of the palace. This was seen by Mahendravarma and he raised sword to bring it upon his neck....</p><p style="text-align: justify;">At that very moment, out came the goat bleating with glee on his sudden freedom. Ahimsa prevailed in Mahendravarma's kingdom.</p><p style="text-align: justify;">And few men vowed to avenge ahimsa...</p><p style="text-align: justify;">So, means or the end...you decide!</p><p style="text-align: justify;"><br /></p><p style="text-align: justify;">By Falguni Madlani</p><p style="text-align: justify;"><br /></p>
