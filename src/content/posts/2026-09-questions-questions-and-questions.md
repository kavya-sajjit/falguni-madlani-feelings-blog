<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="font-size: medium;"><b>&nbsp;Exams and Questions?</b></span></p><p><span style="font-family: georgia;">Recently, I saw a Malayalam movie which showed students trying various means to copy. And to top it, the teenager also taught his dad to cheat for his promotional exam. The story line was nice but I was left wondering .....</span></p><p><span style="font-family: georgia;">Being in the academic field, I regularly come across students who are caught with answers written onto the writing pads, water bottles, etc and lately mobiles and bluetooth devices sneaked inside the exam hall.</span></p><p><span style="font-family: georgia;">Is the movie influencing the people or the peoples' behavior inspiring the movies.</span></p><p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="font-family: georgia;">Cinema or movies have had a great influence on the people specially the young impressionable minds..</span></p><p><span style="font-family: georgia;">I remember the time when many friends of mine started emulating Juhi Chawla's way of speech after 'Qayamat se Qayamat tak' was released. There were news reports of couple trying to commit suicide after parents opposing their love as seen in the film.</span></p><p><span style="font-family: georgia;">Even now teenagers try to model themselves after their favorite stars. With the boom of digital content, there is innumerable protrayal of unethical, irresponsible, superstitious content.&nbsp;</span></p><p><span style="font-family: georgia;">While I do agree&nbsp; that artistic freedom is essential and it helps the society to grow but&nbsp; it should not come at the expense of promoting negative or harmful behavior.</span></p><p><span style="font-family: georgia;">Shouldn't media be more responsible and try to shape perceptions, attitude and behavior? The free use of cuss words amongst friends and besties, scenes of drinking, smoking to look cool will definitely cast an effect on the youngsters who may emulate them to look cool.</span></p><p><span style="font-family: georgia;">&nbsp;Shouldn't we, the consumers of media, remain vigilant and critical of the messages being conveyed? Shouldn't we hold the creators and platforms accountable for their impact on people? Shouln't we ensure that the stories inspire positive change?</span></p><p><span style="font-family: georgia;">But then again what I may consider as cheating and corruption may not be the same for you for example bribing a traffic cop to let go a parking offence.</span></p><p><span style="font-family: georgia;">Who decides?</span></p><p><span style="font-family: georgia;">Media or Us?&nbsp;</span></p><p><span style="font-family: georgia;">By Falguni Madlani</span></p><p><span></span></p><span style="font-family: georgia;">Other Posts:</span><p></p><p><span style="font-family: georgia;"><a href="https://falgunimadlani.blogspot.com/2024/01/hurkle-durkle.html" target="_blank">Hurkle Durkle</a><br /></span></p><p><a href="https://falgunimadlani.blogspot.com/2023/09/my-favorite-ganesha-story.html" target="_blank">My favorite Ganesha story</a><br /></p><p><span style="font-family: georgia;">&nbsp;</span></p><p style="text-align: justify;"><br /></p>
