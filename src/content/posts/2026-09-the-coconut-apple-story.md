<p>&nbsp;The COCONUT APPLE STORY</p><p>Sometimes, stories from the past create such vivid memories that they can never be forgotten. This story is in memory of my mom's best friend, Prema.</p><p>It was the early 1960s, in a small lush green village in Keralam, made even more picturesque by the gentle flow of the River Periyar. A lively gang of five little girls  made this  village  come alive. They were called the Panch Pandavas by their family members</p><p>Those were carefree days—days filled with play, laughter, and simple pleasures, untouched by the distractions of screens. The girls, around 10 to 12 years old, were not only bright in their studies but also full of life—excelling in games, art, dance, and, of course, a fair share of mischief.</p><p>One sunny morning, like any other routine day, they went to school. After a peaceful and calm morning session of classes, the bell for recess rang. These kids excitedly ran out  of their classrooms to play They went out to the nearby fields lined with tall coconut trees.</p><p>Now on that particular day, the farm owner was harvesting coconuts. During harvesting, he came across Pongu that is tender coconut flower found inside the coconut — also known as coconut apple. These are delicious and edible. But when consumed in large quantities or if fermented, they can have an intoxicating effect on some people.</p><p>The girls spotted these treats and started gorging on them enthusiastically—completely unaware of what would follow.</p><p>Cut back to school, when classes resumed, the teacher noticed that five of his students were missing. He was surprised as these girls were also the brightest students of the class. Concerned, he questioned the other children, who mentioned that the girls had gone toward the fields. It was unlikely of them to do so and this made the situation even more unusual.</p><p>As evening approached and the girls did not return home, worry spread quickly. In those times, in the villages, everyone knew everyone. Soon, parents, teachers, and villagers joined in the search for these five pandavas.</p><p>After about an hour search, the five friends were finally found - sitting together, giggling and chatting away, and completely oblivious of their surrounding. On being called out, they were surprised to see the worried  faces of their parents and teachers, not quite understanding the fuss about. As in our culture,     " pehle daat phir pyaar", they were scolded out of relief and then they were gently taken home.</p><p>The next day, the school buzzed with this incident. What began as a simple childhood misadventure soon turned into a legendary tale, retold in school corridors.</p><p>Uff, what a beautiful and a blessed childhood they had—full of innocence, friendship, and unforgettable moments. I wish that  the future generations have such friends and deep bonds in their lives.</p><p>By Falguni Madlani</p>
